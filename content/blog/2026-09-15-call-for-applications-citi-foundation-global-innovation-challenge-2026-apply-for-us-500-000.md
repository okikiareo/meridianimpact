Through the fourth edition of the Global Innovation Challenge, the Citi Foundation plans to provide up to US$25 million in restricted grants to 50 organisations, with each selected organisation receiving US$500,000.

The 2026 challenge, titled “**Preparing Youth for AI**,” will support organisations working on programmes that help low-income youth develop the skills, access and employment opportunities needed in an AI-driven economy. Compare Personal Loans

The Foundation is particularly interested in solutions that combine AI and digital skills with human-centred abilities such as critical thinking, creativity, problem-solving, resilience and adaptability.

The programme is aimed primarily at young people between 15 and 24 years old, although the grant is awarded to the nonprofit organisation running the programme rather than directly to individual beneficiaries.



**What Selected Organisations Will Receive**

* Each selected organisation will receive a restricted US$500,000 grant.

The grant is expected to be paid in two equal instalments:

US$250,000 in 2027

US$250,000 in 2028

* The grant period will run for 24 months, beginning no earlier than June 1, 2027.
* The funding is intended to support the piloting or expansion of programmes that prepare young people for employment in an AI-driven economy.
* Selected organisations will also be part of a learning community, with the Citi Foundation looking for opportunities to support grantees through volunteerism and shared learning.



**What Types of Projects Are Eligible?**

The challenge focuses on three broad areas:

**1. AI and Human-Centred Skills**

Organisations can propose programmes that train young people in areas such as:

* AI fundamentals
* Ethical use of AI
* Prompt engineering
* Digital content creation
* AI-enabled tools
* Problem-solving
* Critical thinking
* Advanced reasoning
* Creativity

The goal is to combine technical AI skills with human-centred skills that can support employment. 

**2. Expanding Digital Access**

Projects can also focus on improving young people’s access to digital resources.

Examples include community hubs with shared devices, open-source AI tools, low-connectivity or offline digital resources, and other approaches that improve digital literacy or access to employment opportunities.

**3. Strengthening Youth Employment Programmes**

Existing youth employability programmes can also be strengthened by integrating AI-enabled tools, training and resources.

**Eligible Countries** 

Nigeria is one of the Citi Foundation’s listed target countries in Africa for the 2026 Global Innovation Challenge. 

Other eligible African countries include Algeria, Cameroon, Côte d’Ivoire, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Egypt, Gabon, Ghana, Kenya, Morocco, Senegal, South Africa, Tanzania, Tunisia, Uganda and Zambia.



**Who Can Apply?**

The opportunity is open to registered nonprofit organisations that meet the Foundation’s eligibility requirements. Applicants must:

* Be registered as a nonprofit entity under local law.
* Have a mission or strategic priorities focused on serving low-income individuals.
* Propose a programme that impacts at least one of the Citi Foundation’s target geographies.
* Be able to provide externally audited financial statements covering part of 2024 or a subsequent year if invited to the full application stage.
* Submit the application in English.
* Organisations using fiscal sponsors are not eligible for this particular funding opportunity.

  **There is no minimum or maximum organisational budget requirement. However, applicants are expected to consider how a US$500,000 grant paid over two years would fit their legal and operational capacity.**



**The Letter of Inquiry (LOI) deadline is October 6, 2026, at 12:00 p.m. New York Time (Eastern Time).**
